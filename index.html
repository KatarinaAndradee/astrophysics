About

I am a Brazilian undergraduate at Northwestern University studying Theatre and Physics & Astronomy. At Northwestern’s Center for Interdisciplinary Exploration and Research in Astrophysics, I investigated what emission-line spectra can reveal about distant galaxies.

My work brings together observational astronomy, quantitative analysis, and science communication. I am especially interested in how careful measurements can recover physical information from the faintest signals.

Research

CIERA · SURG + AYURG · Northwestern University · 2024–present

Spectral stacking and distant galaxies

When individual galaxy spectra are too faint to measure, astronomers combine—or “stack”—many spectra to reveal weak emission lines. Working with 26 KBSS and 24 CECILIA spectra at redshifts of roughly 2–3, I am testing whether the metallicity and gas density measured from a stack accurately represent the galaxies used to create it.

This project was first supported by Northwestern’s Summer Undergraduate Research Grant (SURG), which funds undergraduate research during the summer, and later by an Academic Year Undergraduate Research Grant (AYURG), which supported the project’s continuation during the school year.

I process the spectra through rest-frame alignment, slit-loss correction, wavelength interpolation, and sigma clipping, then compare emission-line ratios in individual and stacked spectra.

Yale Astronomy · Summer 2025

A spectroscopic analysis of the Little Red Dots

Working with Professor Meg Urry and Alessandro Peca, I analyzed JWST spectra of compact, unusually red sources in the early universe. I searched for high-ionization [Ne V] emission, a possible sign of accreting black holes. No individual source yielded a significant [Ne V] detection, so I used the surrounding spectral noise to place quantitative upper limits on the line strength.

High-school research · Citizen science

Buracos Negros Supermassivos no Centro de Galáxias (Supermassive Black Holes at the Centers of Galaxies)

I was privileged to become involved in astrophysical research while I was in high school during the pandemic. Using a computer borrowed from my grandmother, I analyzed wide-field images provided through the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan’s GALAXY CRUISE software, contributing to its search for active galactic nuclei as a citizen scientist.

My work in this program culminated in an article submission titled “Buracos Negros Supermassivos no Centro de Galáxias” to the Brazilian journal Cadernos de Astronomia (“Astronomy Journals”). Through this project, I gained experience with scientific collaboration and writing for publication.

Outreach

CIERA REACH + REACH Further · 2026

Instructional support and one-on-one mentorship

I worked as an instructional assistant for CIERA REACH, helping high-school students build foundational astronomy and research skills. Through REACH Further, I also mentored one student one-on-one through a three-week exoplanet project using real ELODIE spectra to detect 51 Pegasi b with the radial-velocity method. The project culminated in a final presentation.

Dearborn Observatory · Public astronomy

Storytelling under the night sky

At Northwestern’s Dearborn Observatory, I operate the telescope and guide visitors through the night sky. I have also supported solar observing at Dearborn for high-school students through CIERA REACH and for teachers through CIERA’s Research Experiences for Teachers (RET) program.
